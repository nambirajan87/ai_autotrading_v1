Reference & Strategy Guide

Technical Indicators, Scoring & Thresholds

A complete handbook on how indicator weights calculate the final candle score and how signal thresholds gate trading decisions.

1. The Consensus Scoring Architecture

In modern quantitative trading, relying on a single indicator (like an isolated EMA crossover or RSI reading) produces frequent whipsaws. Our system uses a point-scoring consensus system.

Every enabled indicator independently evaluates the current candle and casts its vote:

  • Bullish Condition: Adds positive points equal to its configured weight (+Weight).
  • Bearish Condition: Subtracts points equal to its configured weight (-Weight).
  • Neutral / Conflicting / Inconclusive: Awards 0 points.
Sum of Points = fin_score
The sum of all votes from all enabled indicators on that candle forms the net sentiment score, referred to as fin_score.

2. Indicator Weights & Max Possible Score

What is Indicator Weight?

The Weight represents the voting power (influence) of an indicator:

  • An indicator with Weight = 1 has 1 vote (+1 or -1).
  • An indicator with Weight = 3 has 3 votes (+3 or -3). It is 3 times more influential in deciding whether a trade triggers.

Why is Weight User-Configurable?

Different trading styles prioritize different aspects of price action:

  • Trend-Following Strategy: Assign heavier weights to EMA and MACD (e.g., 2 or 3), and lower weight to mean-reversion filters.
  • Mean-Reversion Strategy: Assign heavier weights to Bollinger Bands, RSI, and Stochastic.
  • Volume Strategy: Prioritize OBV and Volume Ratio.

Dynamic "Max Possible Score"

The Max Possible Score is the sum of all weights across enabled indicators:

Max Possible Score = Σ (Weight of all enabled indicators)

If all indicators are simultaneously bullish, fin_score = +Max Possible Score.
If all indicators are simultaneously bearish, fin_score = -Max Possible Score.

3. How LONG & SHORT Thresholds Work

The LONG Threshold and SHORT Threshold serve as the Gatekeeper (Decision Boundary). They do not alter the fin_score itself; rather, they dictate whether that score warrants opening a position.

Signal Decision Spectrum
← SHORT / SELL ZONE
HOLD / NO TRADE ZONE
LONG / BUY ZONE →
-Max (-11) SHORT Threshold (≤ -5) 0 (Neutral) LONG Threshold (≥ +5) +Max (+11) 
if fin_score >= LONG_Threshold:
    signal = "BUY (LONG)"
elif fin_score <= SHORT_Threshold:
    signal = "SELL (SHORT)"
else:
    signal = "HOLD (Wait / Indecisive Market)"

How Threshold Selection Affects Performance

Setting Style Threshold (Out of 11) Market Behavior Pros & Cons
Conservative LONG: ≥ 8
SHORT: ≤ -8		 Requires near-unanimous agreement across trend, momentum, and volume. High Win Rate, fewer whipsaws, low brokerage fees. Might enter late or miss modest moves.
Balanced (Default) LONG: ≥ 5
SHORT: ≤ -5		 Requires ~50% to 60% consensus of all enabled indicators. Optimal Balance between entry timeliness and confirmation.
Aggressive LONG: ≥ 2
SHORT: ≤ -2		 Enters upon minimal agreement (e.g., just EMA + VWAP agreeing). Early Entries, but High Churn and higher brokerage eating returns.
